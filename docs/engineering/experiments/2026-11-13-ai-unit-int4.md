# AI unit: int4 weights (2026-11-13)

Status: **simulation (`chipsim-ai`, 8 tests) and Yosys synthesis; not on the Nexys Video, not placed.** Step 3 of the weekend plan of
[ADR 0024](../decisions/0024-edge-ai-accelerator.md).

## What it does

CTRL bit 4 (W4, with RAW, Q31 or neither) makes the unit read the weights as int4, two to a byte, the low nibble the lower index, rows one after the
other: the layout of `muriscv_nn_fully_connected_s4` for an even input depth and the output depth of any size (checked below). A row of W is IN/2
bytes and IN must be a multiple of 8 (a row is whole words); W's length for the range check is OUT x IN / 2. Everything else (x, bias, y, the
requantization, the rules for where buffers may lie) is as in int8.

How it is built, in `src/uncore/ai/ai_unit.sv`:

- A word of W now holds eight weights, which the four 8 x 8 multipliers take in **two clocks**: the low four nibbles with x word 2k (clock c+1 after
  the word answers) and the high four with x word 2k+1 (c+2, `Mac2`). The nibbles are sign-extended to bytes in front of the multipliers
  (`WMul`), so no multiplier is added: the cost is a 16-bit mux and a flag.
- The x port serves one word a clock, so the second half's word is read in the clock after the first's, and the bus issues the next word of W only
  after the previous has answered (`IssueCnt == RespCnt`): one word of W every two clocks. **Speed is therefore the same as with int8; what int4
  saves is memory** (half the weight bytes), which is what this RAM lacks. Using the bus's bandwidth to go faster would need two x words a clock
  (a second port, more multipliers) and was not done.
- New registers: W4 and Mac2 (two flip-flops). `Kx` (words of x, IN/4) is separate from `Kw` (words of W per row, IN/8 with W4); the row stride
  of W is IN/2 bytes.

## What was checked

- `ai/mnn/int4.c` (`ai_int4`): **70 layers, the unit's bytes equal `muriscv_nn_fully_connected_s4`'s** in the RAW mode (library requantization in software)
  and in the Q31 mode (56 of them: batch 2 with an output depth not a multiple of 4 cannot be written by the unit's words). Shapes 8 x 1 to 1024 x 4,
  among them 16 x 7, 24 x 33 and 64 x 10, so output depths that are not a multiple of 4 work in the library's layout; batches 1 and 2; random
  input and output offsets, multipliers, shifts, clamps, with and without bias; ten extreme ones (all weights -8, inputs and offsets at the ends).
- `ai/gemv` refuses W4 with IN = 12 and W4 weights that run into the RAM part of the input area.
- The same network as `digits.c`, with int4 weights (`digits.c -DDIGITS_W4`, `ai_digits4`): the library's s4 function, the unit RAW and the unit Q31
  agree on every layer of the first eight samples, and the 200 samples give the host's classes (0 different).
- Lint (`asic_soc_rv32imac_ai`, `asic_soc_rv32imac`, and `lint-wally`): clean. `make chipsim-ai` with the seven tests that existed plus `ai_int4`: 7 of 7 pass; `ai_gemv` with the two new refusals and `ai_digits4` were run afterwards on their own (the filter `ai_gemv|ai_digits4`): 2 of 2 pass.

## Accuracy (the trained digits network, `model/train_digits.py --bits 4`)

| Weights | Test set, 597 samples | Embedded samples (200) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| float | 0.9866 | |
| int8, symmetric per tensor | 0.9866 | 0.9900 (198 correct) |
| int4 after training (post-training quantization, clipping chosen by least squared error) | 0.9749 | |
| int4 with quantization-aware fine-tuning (60 epochs, straight-through) | **0.9866** | 0.9900 (198 correct) |

Post-training int4 loses seven samples of 597; fine-tuning with the quantized weights in the forward pass gets all of them back on this task (digits
is easy: the result is not a promise for other networks). On the chip the 200 samples are classified like the host's integer model.

## Cycles and bytes

| | int8 | int4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 64 x 64 layer, unit in Q31, unit's counter | 1,539 | 1,539 |
| weight bytes of that layer | 4,096 | 2,048 |
| weights of the 64-64-32-10 network | 6,464 | 3,232 |
| digits network, one inference on the unit (Q31, the core's `mcycle`) | 4,201 | 4,465 |
| digits network, one inference in the library on the core | 135,957 | 193,718 |

The unit is 6 % slower per inference with int4 (the layers are short, 32 to 64 inputs); the cause was not analysed. The library's scalar s4 code is
42 % slower than its s8 code on the core (it unpacks nibbles one by one), so the unit's gain over the library grows: 43 times.
